Freelance invoice tracking: a simple weekly workflow

A lightweight way to know which invoices are due, partly paid or overdue without turning a spreadsheet into an accounting system.

Use one row per invoice

Use a separate row for every invoice, even when the same client has several projects. Record the client, project, invoice amount, due date, paid amount and your next follow-up date. This makes each outstanding payment visible on its own.

Handle partial payments without losing the balance

Keep the full invoice amount unchanged. When money arrives, update only the paid amount. The difference is the balance still outstanding. Replacing the invoice total with the payment received hides what remains unpaid.

Review once a week

  1. Filter for invoices with a balance greater than zero.
  2. Check whether the due date has passed.
  3. Set a specific next follow-up date for each open invoice.
  4. Review your collected and outstanding totals before taking new work.

What overdue means

An invoice is overdue when it has a due date in the past and a remaining balance. A missing due date should be treated as information you still need to add, not as an overdue invoice.

Use a tracker only for its intended job

A tracker can organize your records and follow-ups. It cannot create legal invoices, calculate tax, send reminders or replace accounting advice.
